--- Document 1: Utopia Planitia Polygonal Terrain ---
Polygonal terrain in Utopia Planitia, Mars, exhibits a characteristic network
of low-centred polygons ranging from 10 to 300 metres in diameter.  High-
resolution imagery from HiRISE and CTX reveals that the polygon boundaries
are associated with shallow troughs that indicate subsurface ice-rich permafrost.
The formation mechanism is analogous to thermal contraction cracking observed
in periglacial environments on Earth, particularly in the Siberian lowlands and
the Canadian Arctic tundra.  Seasonal temperature cycles on Mars drive repeated
expansion and contraction of near-surface regolith, producing orthogonal crack
networks over geological timescales.

--- Document 2: Jarosite and Aqueous History ---
The mineral jarosite was first identified on Mars by the Opportunity rover at
Meridiani Planum using the Mossbauer spectrometer.  Jarosite (KFe3(SO4)2(OH)6)
is an iron-potassium sulphate mineral that forms exclusively under acidic and
oxidising aqueous conditions, with pH below 3.  On Earth, jarosite is a common
secondary mineral in acid mine drainage environments and is found in weathered
sulphide deposits in Rio Tinto, Spain, which serves as a key Mars analog site.
The presence of jarosite in Meridiani Planum deposits provides strong evidence
for the existence of acidic liquid water during the Noachian to Hesperian
transition on Mars.

--- Document 3: Mars–Earth Analog Studies Overview ---
Comparative planetology relies on identifying terrestrial analog environments
that replicate the physical, chemical, and morphological conditions of Martian
surface features.  Key analog sites include: (1) the Atacama Desert in Chile,
which models present-day Mars desiccation and UV radiation conditions;
(2) the McMurdo Dry Valleys in Antarctica, which serve as periglacial analogs
for Martian polar processes; (3) the Kamchatka Peninsula in Russia, which
provides hydrothermal system analogs for ancient Martian volcanic activity;
and (4) Lanzarote, Spain, which models lava tube environments relevant to
potential Martian subsurface habitats.
